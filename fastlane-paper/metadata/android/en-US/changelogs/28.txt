A phone correcting its own clock could hand a player time or take it away, and this was true of every release so far. Timing came from wall-clock time, so an NTP sync, a manual change or a carrier correction landed straight on whoever was on move: backwards gave them the drift, forwards deducted the whole gap in one tick and could flag somebody with minutes in hand. It now uses a clock that counts from boot and cannot be set by anyone.
The clock no longer redraws a hundred times a second. This build shows seconds and never hundredths, so it was ticking a hundred times a second to redraw digits that had not changed; it now ticks ten times a second — which matters more here than anywhere else, since an E-Ink screen is asked to do the redrawing.
Autosave runs every fifteen seconds instead of every five. At five it was around 1,400 database writes across a two-hour game, for a recovery that almost never happens.
