A phone correcting its own clock could hand a player time or take it away, and this was true of every release so far. Timing came from wall-clock time, so an NTP sync, a manual change or a carrier correction landed straight on whoever was on move: backwards gave them the drift, forwards deducted the whole gap in one tick and could flag somebody with minutes in hand. It now uses a clock that counts from boot and cannot be set by anyone. Per-move times in an exported PGN are measured the same way.
The clock no longer redraws a hundred times a second. The tick follows the display — fast only while hundredths are showing, ten times slower otherwise, which is most of most games. That is battery saved on a device meant to sit awake on a table for hours, and accuracy is unchanged.
Autosave runs every fifteen seconds instead of every five, and a Bluetooth scan stops itself after thirty seconds instead of running for as long as the app is open.
Link board: an error no longer takes the scan button away. Scanning with Bluetooth off told you to enable it and then left you no way to try again; the button is back whenever nothing is connected, and it clears a failed attempt on all three transports first.
Link board: a board going out of range used to keep one of the phone's Bluetooth connection slots for good. A few disconnections and no further Bluetooth connection would succeed, in any app, until the phone was restarted.
Link board: a move read from the board is dropped when the board goes away, instead of waiting to be recorded against a press minutes later or in another game entirely.
Link board: reconnecting no longer leaks the previous connection on any of the three transports, an acknowledgement the board is owed can no longer be silently dropped, and two intermittent races on the Bluetooth Classic path are fixed.
Link board: a Certabo now says it is waiting for the pieces to be set up, rather than sitting connected and silent, and it is recognised over USB as well as Bluetooth.
